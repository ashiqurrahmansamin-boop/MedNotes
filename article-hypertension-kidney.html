Why Does Hypertension Damage the Kidneys?

6 min read · MedNotes

Long-standing high blood pressure can contribute to progressive kidney injury. The relationship is important because the kidneys help regulate blood pressure, so kidney disease and hypertension can reinforce one another.

1. Pressure on small renal vessels

Persistent elevation of arterial pressure exposes small renal arteries and arterioles to increased mechanical stress. Over time, vascular remodeling and narrowing can reduce the normal blood supply to parts of the kidney.

2. Glomerular stress

Changes in renal blood flow and pressure can increase stress within glomerular capillaries. Continued injury may contribute to glomerular structural changes and loss of functioning filtration units.

3. A reinforcing cycle

As kidney function declines, the kidney's ability to regulate sodium, fluid balance and blood pressure can become impaired. This can make hypertension more difficult to control and potentially accelerate further kidney damage.

Key takeaway: Think of hypertension and chronic kidney disease as conditions that can influence one another. Persistent blood-pressure control is therefore an important part of kidney-protection strategies.
